Response expectations

Support Priority Levels

Every support request is assigned a priority based on business impact. Response targets are goals — not guaranteed resolution times.

Priority: Standard

General Requests

Non-urgent issues, how-to questions, and minor inconveniences that do not interrupt business operations.

Priority: High

Operational Impact

Issues affecting one or more users that reduce productivity but do not halt business operations entirely.

Priority: Critical Emergency

Business Stopped

Complete system failure, active security incident, or loss of access that prevents business from operating.

Response means we are working on it. A response may include reviewing the request, starting triage, requesting required information, communicating next steps, or beginning remediation. Support Policy

Support Expectations.

Understanding what a response means, and what to expect from the support process.

How support works

Response Does Not Mean Resolution.

When Forged Legacy acknowledges or responds to a support request, that response may include any of the following actions — not necessarily a completed fix.

Step 1

Request Review

We review the ticket and assess the reported issue.

Step 2

Triage

We determine priority, impact, and initial steps.

Step 3

Information Gathering

We may request additional details to properly diagnose the issue.

Step 4

Communication

We communicate next steps, estimated timelines, and any constraints.

Step 5

Remediation

Active work begins. Resolution time depends on complexity.

Response time targets are goals, not guarantees. Actual resolution time depends on the nature, complexity, and root cause of the issue. Some problems are resolved in minutes. Others require vendor coordination, parts procurement, or extended diagnosis. We communicate status throughout the process.

How to Submit a Support Request

All support requests must be submitted through the approved channels defined in your managed services agreement. Approved channels may include a support ticket portal, a dedicated support email, or a support phone number.

Personal text messages, social media messages, and unofficial contact methods are not approved support channels and do not guarantee a response or create a support obligation. Use your designated support channel to ensure your request is properly tracked and prioritized.

Emergency criteria

What Qualifies as an After-Hours Emergency.

Not every technology problem is an emergency. True after-hours emergencies are events that completely prevent business operations and cannot wait until the next business day.

Complete Internet Outage

Total loss of internet connectivity affecting all business operations — not a slow connection or single device issue.

Business-Critical System Down

A core business system is unavailable for multiple users and business operations cannot continue without it.

Active Cyber Incident

Suspected ransomware, active breach, or security incident requiring immediate containment action.

Major Network Outage

Complete network failure affecting multiple users or locations — not isolated connectivity problems.

Critical Infrastructure Failure

Core server, firewall, or network appliance failure that stops business from operating.

When in doubt, wait until business hours. If your business can operate — even in a reduced capacity — the issue should be submitted through standard support channels the next business day. After-hours emergency response is reserved for complete operational failures, not inconveniences.
